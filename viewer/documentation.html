Project goal

This project organizes Philippine FDA medicine records and proposes names that may look or sound alike. It helps a human reviewer find candidates faster. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or prove that a medication error will occur.

Scope: sound-alike first

Look-alike (spelling) similarity is largely the same no matter who reads a name, so it needs little localization. Sound-alike (pronunciation) similarity depends on how a name is spoken, which is language-specific. Because this project's contribution is localizing detection to Cebuano and the wider Philippine context, it concentrates on the sound-alike dimension, where localization changes the result; the look-alike score is kept but largely inherited from existing measures.

What data we collect

The product registry supplies registration number, published generic name, published brand name, strength, dosage form, classification, manufacturer, country, issuance date and expiry date. When an official leaflet is available, a separate process extracts sourced description and indication text with page references.

Important: 31,208 registration records do not mean 31,208 unique drugs. Strengths, forms, manufacturers, renewals and brands can create separate registrations.

Step-by-step workflow

  1. Preserve the source.Save each source separately and retain the registration number.
  2. Clean formatting.Standardize whitespace and capitalization without silently correcting published names.
  3. Create stable entities.Build generic-formulation and brand-name identifiers, linked back to registrations.
  4. Generate possible pairs.Use blocking keys so plausible names are compared without testing every possible combination.
  5. Build documented pronunciation paths.Keep English dictionary/G2P, Cebuano direct-reading, and bounded adaptation routes separate; record each path’s IPA, source, evidence, coverage, and cost.
  6. Calculate similarity signals.Measure spelling, IPA phonemes, IPA-syllable chunks, optional known stress, letter bigrams, and a cost-adjusted pronunciation lattice score.
  7. Separate obvious data cases.Place normalization duplicates and probable spelling/source variants in their own queues.
  8. Human review.Check ingredients, strengths, uses, registrations and external evidence before applying a final label.

Algorithms used

Jaro–Winkler

Scores spelling similarity from 0 to 1 and gives extra weight to a shared beginning.

Levenshtein edit distance

Counts how many character insertions, deletions or substitutions change one name into another.

Soundex

Creates a rough phonetic code. It is useful as a supporting signal, not proof of pronunciation.

Cebuano Editex

A phonetic edit distance where substituting within a Cebuano-equivalent letter group (e/i/y, o/u/w, b/v, p/f, c/k/q, s/z, m/n) costs less than an unrelated substitution. Better suited to sound-alike than spelling-oriented measures. Exposed as a signal; not yet blended into the ranking score.

Head/tail (htco)

Shared letters in the first two and last two positions, after the Japanese PMDA drug-name similarity index — confusion often concentrates at a name's beginning and ending. Exposed as a supporting signal.

Pronunciation lattice

Keeps several costed routes instead of declaring one local pronunciation correct: curated IPA, exact CMUdict entries, eSpeak NG contextual G2P for unknown names, optional English Epitran/Flite, coverage-checked Cebuano Epitran direct reading, and bounded adaptations. ceb-Latn is a spelling-reading hypothesis, not an accent model.

IPA-syllable alignment

Groups IPA segments around vowel nuclei and aligns spoken-like chunks. Stress is used only when a selected source provides it; Epitran stress is unknown rather than guessed.

Weighted phoneme alignment

Compares IPA segments. Controlled pairs such as f/p, v/b, z/s, e/i and o/u receive smaller substitution penalties than unrelated sounds.

Orthographic bigrams

Measures overlap between two-character chunks. It preserves a spelling signal alongside the experimental speech features.

Blocking

Groups names by spelling plus coarse IPA-derived prefixes, suffixes, and length buckets before scoring. This lets a phonetically similar but differently spelled pair reach full comparison.

Contextual browser pronunciation

English has more spoken vowels than five vowel letters, and a spelling can change with nearby letters, syllable structure, morphology, or a lexical exception. The browser therefore uses bundled eSpeak NG grapheme-to-phoneme rules instead of trying to maintain one regular expression for every English vowel pattern.

What “rule-based” means here: eSpeak NG combines an exception dictionary with ordered rules that inspect preceding and following context. Unknown drug-like names fall through to those general rules. This is broader than a fixed word lookup, but a coined brand still needs speaker or manufacturer validation.

The aligned route also remains visible when no vowel needs changing. Examples include ball → bol, late → leyt, head → hed, and cloud → klawd. Contextual repairs include basket → basket, note → not, topic → topik, model → model, umbrella → ambrela, uranus → yoreynos, and idea → aydiya. Regression cases such as omeprazole, azithromycin, furosemide, and aspirin verify that internal silent vowels or vowel-like y no longer make the local route disappear.

The old browser-only word table and normal-path blanket e→i/o→u rewrites were removed. Free-text search, the Pronunciation Lab, product suggestions, and chat name resolution now call the same browser G2P module. Registered-name paths and saved pair scores still come from the richer Python batch lattice and remain authoritative.

How the batch score is combined

The dashboard exposes every component so a reviewer can see why a pair surfaced. The weights below are hand-set engineering choices for candidate ordering; they were not learned from labeled errors and must not be interpreted as accuracy or probability.

Raw pronunciation-path score

When stress is unknown: 67% weighted IPA phonemes + 33% IPA-syllable alignment. When both paths have known stress: 60% phonemes + 30% syllables + 10% stress.

Cost-adjusted lattice score

The selected score is the best raw path-pair value after subtracting a fixed penalty for both path costs. The raw maximum remains visible for audit, but it does not outrank a more credible route just because two speculative paths match.

Multi-feature ensemble

52% cost-adjusted lattice + 23% phonemes + 17% syllables + 8% spelling bigrams. It is a fixed experimental ranking feature, not a probability.

Numeric distinction penalty

When the letter-name base is almost identical but strength or version numbers differ, the final ensemble loses 0.10; it loses 0.18 when the letter base is exactly equal. Near-identical numeric variants stay in the full screening data but are moved out of the main high/medium review queue.

Browser preview signals

The Cebuano tools may show browser-only Editex and head/tail suggestions for free text. They are not the authoritative batch result for a registered medicine name.

Why it is called experimental: no Cebuano medicine-name confusion gold set has been used to estimate sensitivity, specificity, precision or recall. The interface therefore says “candidate,” never “verified,” based on this score.

How the pair pages fit together

Pair Review

The complete high/medium human-review queue. Each pair can be retained through spelling, general phonetic, or experimental Cebuano-aware evidence.

Cebuano pairs

A filtered pair-review page showing candidates where Cebuano-aware transform, pronunciation lattice, or multi-feature pronunciation signals contributed to the match.

Cebuano tools

A focused workspace for approximate reading search, two-name comparison, and one-drug pronunciation-path exploration. It uses the same official medicine names, but exposes the Cebuano-aware reading-path signals more directly.

Pronunciation lab

A free-form tool for made-up or proposed names. Its bundled eSpeak NG engine generates contextual English IPA, then shows local phone adaptations and direct-spelling recovery as separate, costed hypotheses. It does not claim CMUdict evidence or a verified local pronunciation.

Review classifications

Verified LASA pair

Both medicines and a meaningful confusion risk have been confirmed by documented human review.

Potential LASA pair

Names are similar, but confusion evidence has not been established.

Spelling variant or probable typo

One published entry may be a misspelling or source-field problem requiring verification.

Normalization duplicate

The names become equal after harmless formatting differences are removed.

False positive

A human reviewer documented that the algorithmic match is unlikely to represent meaningful confusion.

Future experiments—not implemented or validated yet

Cebuano medicine-pronunciation lexicon

Collect consented, speaker-provided pronunciations of medicine names instead of relying only on hand-written reading rules.

Learned ranking weights

Replace hand-set weights only after pharmacists create a sufficiently large, consistently labeled review set.

Speech-alternative evaluation

Measure whether multiple microphone transcripts improve retrieval without creating too many unrelated suggestions.

Ingredient and product guardrails

Use ingredient, strength, route and dosage form to explain product relationships and down-rank obvious same-product/version cases—not to declare LASA automatically.

Uncertainty calibration

After validation data exists, convert raw ranking scores into interpretable confidence bands with documented error rates.

Dialect and speaker variation study

Test whether controlled pronunciation paths represent real variation across Cebuano speakers without assigning stereotypes to language groups.

Limitations and safety rules

  • A high similarity score does not prove clinical confusion.
  • The PH FDA source can contain misspellings, reversed fields or missing leaflets.
  • OCR text can be wrong and must retain a review flag and page reference.
  • An LLM may help organize text but must not invent missing indications or descriptions.
  • Pronunciation paths are hypotheses, not claims about how every Cebuano speaker talks.
  • The fixed weights are not trained, calibrated or clinically validated.
  • Pairs are the review unit; groups only summarize multiple pair relationships.
